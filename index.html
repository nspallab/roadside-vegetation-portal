Roadside Vegetation Portal is a GIS-based information and operations-planning platform for drone-based herbicide spraying along state highways. It helps transportation agencies track roadside vegetation, find sites that need control, and plan drone spraying, mowing, and inspection routes that are safe, efficient and compliant.

Vegetation Information

Geo-coded roadside vegetation data: species, height, density, growth stage, photos, and control history.

Operations Planning

Recommend mowing, drone spraying or inspection, and plan crew routes with time and herbicide estimates.

Drone Technology

Market review of spraying drones, payloads and productivity, and the FAA rules that apply.

Operational Guidelines

Equipment, licensing and step-by-step guidance so DOTs can adopt drones in their vegetation programs.

        Rules that shape operations

        FAA Part 107

        Remote pilot certificate for commercial drone flights. Recurrent training every 24 months. Flights below 400 ft and within visual line of sight unless waived.

        FAA Part 137

        Agricultural aircraft operator certificate, required to dispense herbicide from a drone. Spraying drones fly with a pilot and a visual observer, one pilot per drone.

        Equipment eligibility

        NDAA-compliant, U.S.-available platforms are preferred. Foreign-made drones such as DJI are surveyed for comparison in case restrictions change. BVLOS rules (Part 108) are pending.

        Wind is a key constraint: many spraying drones operate safely only below about 6 m/s (13.5 mph), and Midwest winds often exceed that.
