How it works

  1. 1

    Collect

    Drone and ground inspections record vegetation type, height, weed cover and growth stage. Field crews upload updates as CSV or GeoJSON.

  2. 2

    Screen

    Adjustable triggers flag the sites that need control and rank them by priority: height, weed cover, sight distance, traffic and time since inspection.

  3. 3

    Recommend

    Each flagged site gets an action: drone spot spraying, mowing or further inspection. Planners can change any of them.

  4. 4

    Plan

    Crews are routed from IDOT maintenance yards. The timeline plays the work day with travel, work time and herbicide use.

        Rules that shape operations

        FAA Part 107

        Remote pilot certificate for commercial drone flights. Recurrent training every 24 months. Flights below 400 ft and within visual line of sight unless waived.

        FAA Part 137

        Agricultural aircraft operator certificate, required to dispense herbicide from a drone. Spraying drones fly with a pilot and a visual observer, one pilot per drone.

        Equipment eligibility

        NDAA-compliant, U.S.-available platforms are preferred. Foreign-made drones such as DJI are surveyed for comparison in case restrictions change. BVLOS rules (Part 108) are pending.

        Wind is a key constraint: many spraying drones operate safely only below about 6 m/s (13.5 mph), and Midwest winds often exceed that.
